FAQ

Questions workshops ask.

We run on Pastel. Does Jobbook replace it?

No. Pastel stays your book of record for quotes, POs and invoices. Jobbook does everything in front of Pastel — the enquiry, the material list, the supplier prices, the order at the quoted ceiling, the shop floor — and hands Pastel the lines in its own column order. PO and invoice numbers are typed back so the job and the paperwork sit together.

It's one person. What if he disappears?

The hosting is in your name, the code is in your repository, the documentation is in your drive, and every screen is standard React and PostgreSQL. Any competent developer picks it up in a day. That is deliberate: Jobbook is built so you never depend on PUSH AI to keep running.

Our people aren't computer people.

Neither were the people it was built with. Each screen is shaped with the person who does that job, on a test copy, before it goes live: the buyer's screen with the buyer, the foreman's board with the foreman. Big buttons, plain words, in the language the floor speaks, and nothing you can break by pressing.

Our suppliers are in a spreadsheet one person keeps.

That's where every workshop starts. The last import took 283 suppliers from one spreadsheet into a register grouped the way the buyer thinks, with every change logged. Her own words for each supplier stayed next to the group.

How long until we're on it?

Front office and Quoting go live in weeks, not quarters. Your suppliers, clients, people and forms are loaded for you. Each further module is a two-week sprint, in the order your own work demands. The date comes from real capacity, and once confirmed it holds.

Can we start with just one part?

Yes. Most workshops start with Quoting and Procurement, because that is where the money leaks — prices that went stale between quote and order. The shop floor follows when the office is settled.

We're not in the Vaal. Does that matter?

No. Jobbook runs in the browser and is hosted on AWS, so a workshop in Pretoria, Durban or Cape Town runs it the same way. The scoping morning is in person across the Vaal and Gauteng and on screen everywhere else; the test copy, the walk-through and the training work the same either way. Africa is next on the map.

Phones and tablets on the floor?

Yes, and nothing to install. Jobbook runs in the browser, so the owner sees the boards and approves what's waiting from his phone, in the bakkie or at home. The shop runs on one tablet per bay — the foreman's board, clock-ins, QC and "ready" — rather than a device per person, so a R3 000 tablet and a screen protector does a bay. The office keeps the PC for quoting, prices and orders. Same system, same job, three screens.

Load-shedding and no signal on site?

Built to keep working on a phone with unreliable signal, to notify the team over WhatsApp, and hosted on AWS with daily backups. When the power comes back, so does the office — nothing in a book that got left at the counter.

Why "PUSH AI" if the page never mentions AI?

Because the AI isn't the product; the blue book on a screen is. Two places it sits: behind the scenes, where AI tooling builds Jobbook fast under a human gate — and ahead of you, where Jobbook records every job, price and clock-in from day one so that, after a few months, it can tell you which quotes you'll win, how long a job really takes and which supplier will be late. No rebuild, no data science project. It's just ready when you are.

Who builds it, and is AI involved?

PUSH AI is a senior-led practice: Len Klopper, who ran delivery for Nedbank, Discovery, Investec and MTN, now applied to one trade. AI development tooling does the heavy lifting under that gate; a person reads every change before it reaches your live system, and nothing is called done until it has been walked as every role on the test copy.
